# Approval-ready outreach

Use only owner-approved recipients with a legitimate professional basis. Keep
individual sends below support capacity. Do not attach source drawings or large
model files to cold messages.

## Sequence A — direct design partner

### Email 1 — one representative sheet

Subject: `Could we turn one of your legacy plans into a reviewable BIM model?`

Hi `[First name]`,

I am opening a small founding cohort for Dajoong, our drawing-to-BIM compiler.
It is for teams that still rebuild existing conditions from floor-plan PDFs.

For one representative sheet, we will deliver a private, review-gated IFC/GLB
model and a Build Sheet showing what the system created, what still needs human
review, and which source and model produced every result.

This is not a request to publish your drawing. Public use and model-training
permission are separate, optional decisions.

Would a 20-minute review of one floor be useful for `[their use case]`?

Paul Cho

## Sequence B — BIM consultant channel

### Partner invitation

Subject: `A first-pass drawing-to-BIM tool that keeps the reviewer in control`

Hi `[First name]`,

I am looking for five BIM consultants to help test a different delivery model.

consultant keeps the client relationship and supervises final acceptance.

repeatable delivery agreement — not a referral link dropped on your website.

Would you be open to testing one difficult plan together?

Paul Cho

## Discovery call outline — 20 minutes

1. `2 min` — What decision requires the model?
2. `4 min` — Current source set, scale, revisions, and confidentiality
3. `6 min` — Live split-view review of the highest-risk entities
4. `4 min` — IFC/GLB handoff and downstream application
5. `3 min` — Building-level scope and acceptance owner
6. `1 min` — Agree one next step or close the pilot

Do not spend the call presenting generic AI architecture.
